# Portfolio copy style

The portfolio should sound like a senior operator describing real work to another senior operator.

## Voice

- Use plain English.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over abstract labels.
- Say what was built, what changed and what the result was.
- Use first person when the point is Josh's contribution.
- Keep technical detail when it proves something, but explain it in normal language.
- Let metrics do the work. Do not decorate them with grand claims.

## Avoid

Do not use language such as:

- "the engineering is open to inspect"
- "the commercial logic"
- "every capability"
- "what proves it"
- "journey" when "flow", "process" or the actual action is clearer
- "surface" when "page", "screen", "product" or "interface" is meant
- "capability" when "feature" or the actual function is meant
- "bounded roles", "governed learning", "control plane", "handoff packets" or similar internal build terminology in customer-facing copy unless the term is genuinely necessary
- repeated explanations that something is "a reference implementation"
- descriptions of agent orchestration when the reader needs to know what Josh built
- inflated transition phrases such as "turns X into Y" when a direct sentence works

## Prefer

- "View source" instead of "Inspect the source"
- "The problem" instead of "Why it exists"
- "Key features" instead of "Every capability"
- "Tech" instead of "Built with"
- "Proof" instead of "What proves it"
- "Business model" instead of "The commercial logic"
- "I built..." / "I led..." / "I defined..." instead of passive process language

## Test every paragraph

Before shipping copy, ask:

1. Would a real person say this out loud?
2. Does it name the actual thing rather than an abstraction around it?
3. Is the sentence shorter without losing a fact?
4. Is process detail crowding out the outcome?
5. If a technical term stays, does it earn its place?

If the answer to any of those is no, rewrite it.
